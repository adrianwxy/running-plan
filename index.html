§ One — The Week

The shape of a training week.

Quality on Wednesday. Easy on Friday. Long on Sunday. Strength bookends the running so the legs lift fresh on Tuesday and recover into Saturday.

Mon Rest Walk, mobility, or nothing at all.
Tue Strength A PF-focused. Heavy slow calf raises + single-leg work.
Wed Quality Run Threshold session. 4–7K total incl. 1K WU.
Thu Rest Real recovery. Light core only if anything.
Fri Easy + Strength B 5–10K easy run, then general lifting same day.
Sat Rest Non-negotiable. Bridge day before long run.
Sun Long Run 10–20K easy. Conversational the whole way.
§ Two — Paces

Run by feel, verified by pace.

These are the paces that match your current fitness. Heart rate is the truer guide on any given day — let it lead, especially on hot or tired days.

Easy 5:40 – 6:00 /km Conversational. Full sentences. Z2.
Long Run 5:40 – 6:00 /km Same as easy. Resist the urge to go faster.
Threshold 4:45 – 4:54 /km Comfortably uncomfortable. Z4. Short phrases only.
5K Race 4:32 /km Reference point — current PR pace.
Marathon Goal 4:15 /km Future pace. Not used in Phase 0.
§ Three — The Runs

Three runs, three distinct purposes.

Wednesday · Quality

Threshold Session

Alternate weekly between continuous and cruise intervals. Always 1K easy warm-up first, optional 1K cool-down if time allows.

  • Format A Continuous: 1K WU → 20–25 min @ threshold (4:48/km) → done
  • Format B Cruise: 1K WU → 2 × 12 min @ 4:45/km, 2-min jog between
  • Format C Cruise: 1K WU → 3 × 8 min @ 4:45/km, 90s jog between
  • Cue Should feel hard but sustainable. Z4 only. No Z5.
Friday · Aerobic

Easy Run

The most undervalued run of the week. Builds the mitochondrial base that makes threshold pace possible.

  • Volume 5–10 km, building gradually within the cycle
  • Pace 5:40–6:00/km. Slower is fine. Faster is not.
  • HR Stay in Zone 2. If breathing climbs, slow down.
  • After Strength Session B, same day
Sunday · Long

Long Run

The single most important running adaptation in Phase 0. Extend by ~1K every 2–3 weeks during build weeks.

  • Volume 10–20 km, progressing across the cycle
  • Pace Conversational throughout. ~5:40–6:00/km.
  • Goal Time on feet. Don't race it. Don't end it tired.
  • Future Will progress to 28–32K before marathon-specific work begins
§ Four — Strength

Two sessions, different jobs.

Tuesday is for the plantar fascia and the small stabilizers. Friday is for general posterior chain and movement quality. Both end in calf raises, because the foot is the foundation of everything.

Session A — Plantar Fascia & Stability

Tuesday
Heavy Slow Calf Raise — Toes Extended ★ priority 3 × 12 · every other day

The Rathleff protocol. Stand on a step with a rolled towel under your toes (this is the key — toes must be dorsiflexed). Rise up over 3 seconds, pause 2 seconds at top, lower over 3 seconds. Single leg if possible. Add weight (backpack, then dumbbells) as it gets easier. This is the exercise.

Watch demonstration
Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift 3 × 8 each leg

Hinge at the hip on one leg, opposite leg extending behind for balance. Bodyweight first, then add a light dumbbell in the opposite hand. Builds posterior chain and proprioception — every running stride is a unilateral load.

Watch demonstration
Side-Lying Leg Raise 3 × 15 each side

Glute medius activation. Lie on your side, lift the top leg slowly, lower under control. Weak hips let the knee collapse inward, which loads the medial foot — upstream cause of PF.

Watch demonstration
Single-Leg Balance 3 × 30 sec each

Stand on one leg, eyes open. Progress to eyes closed when easy, then add a soft surface (folded towel). Trains the small stabilizing muscles of the foot and ankle.

Watch demonstration

Session B — General Strength

Friday · post-run
Goblet Squat 3 × 10

Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell at your chest. Squat to comfortable depth, knees tracking over toes. Builds quads, glutes, and core in a runner-friendly pattern. Start with bodyweight if needed.

Watch demonstration
Bulgarian Split Squat 3 × 8 each leg

Rear foot elevated on a bench or step, front foot forward. Lower the back knee toward the floor, drive up through the front foot. The single most translatable strength exercise for distance running.

Watch demonstration
Heavy Slow Calf Raise (round 2) 3 × 12

Same as Tuesday — toes extended on a step, 3 sec up, 2 sec pause, 3 sec down. Two sessions per week is the proven cadence for tendon/fascia adaptation.

Watch demonstration
Banded Lateral Walk 2 × 15 each direction

Resistance band around ankles or above knees, athletic stance, side-step against the band's resistance. Hits glute med directly. A fatigued, jelly-like burn at the side of the hip is the goal.

Watch demonstration
Plank 3 × 45 sec

Forearms on the floor, body in a straight line from heels to head. Don't let the hips sag. Quality over duration — 45 seconds clean beats 90 seconds sloppy.

Watch demonstration
Dead Bug 3 × 10 each side

Lying on your back, lower opposite arm and leg slowly while keeping your low back pressed into the floor. Anti-extension core work — what you actually need as a runner.

§ Five — PF Maintenance

Daily care for the fascia.

These take five minutes total, daily. Not training — maintenance. The foot bath is good. These additions make it better.

Morning Routine

Before getting out of bed
  1. 10 slow ankle circles each direction (under the covers)
  2. 10 toe scrunches — curl toes toward sole, release
  3. Stand carefully, then your foot bath as planned
  4. 5 min easy walking before any running pace

Post-Run Routine

Within 30 min of finishing
  1. Frozen water bottle under the foot, 5 min rolling
  2. Calf stretch: against the wall, 30 sec each side
  3. Soleus stretch: same wall, knee bent, 30 sec each side
  4. Hydrate, eat, get on with your day
§ Six — The Cycle

Two weeks build, one week back off.

Tendons and fascia adapt slower than muscle. The deload week isn't a rest week — it's the week the tissue actually gets stronger.

01
Build
Lower end of distance ranges. Easy 5K, long 10K, quality 4K total.
02
Build
Push toward upper end. Easy 8K, long 15K, quality 5–6K total.
03
Deload
Pull back ~30%. Easy 5K, long 10K, quality kept short and sharp. Strength volume halved.

Then repeat — and each new cycle nudges the upper bound a little further than the last. Slowly. Long-run ceiling moves from 20K → 25K → 28K over months, not weeks.

§ Seven — Hold Onto These

The principles that keep this working.

i
Easy means easy. If you can't speak in full sentences on Friday or Sunday, you're running too hard. The aerobic base is built at paces that feel almost too slow.
ii
One quality session per week. No more. The threshold run is the engine. Adding a second hard session on three days/week is how runners with PF history get hurt.
iii
Lift fresh, run threshold next day. Tuesday strength → Wednesday quality. Never the other way. Heavy load on fatigued tissue is where injuries live.
iv
Saturday is sacred. Full rest between Friday strength and Sunday long. This single off-day protects the long run more than any extra mileage would build it.
v
Load before complexity. Don't collect new exercises. Add weight to the ones you already do. Bodyweight squat → goblet squat → loaded goblet squat.
vi
Consistency beats heroic. Twelve months of three good runs per week beats six weeks of five before another PF flare. The marathon rewards the patient.
