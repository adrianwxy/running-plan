§ One — The Week

The shape of a training week.

Four running days, two lifting days, one full rest day. Tuesday and Wednesday can swap for weather — when that happens, Monday's lift is skipped that week rather than shuffled around. Thursday stays light — that's an office day. Monday stays light too, on purpose: new stimulus for the legs, kept far enough ahead of Wednesday's threshold to fully clear.

Mon Strength B Light strength, fresh legs. Calf raises, band work, core.
Tue Easy Run Zone 2, conversational. Swaps with Wed if weather calls for it.
Wed Threshold WU + threshold + CD. Swaps with Tue if needed.
Thu Strength A · AM PF prehab, ~15–20 min. Light on purpose — office day.
Fri Easy Run Zone 2, conversational. Same rules as Tuesday.
Sat Long Run Zone 2 base, then a surge to Zone 3/low-Zone 4 for the close.
Sun Rest Full rest. Long run was yesterday — let it settle.
§ Two — Zones & Paces

Heart rate leads, pace follows.

Zones govern effort now, not the other way around. Every pace below is just what a heart rate target happens to produce right now — track the trend in that number over the coming months, not the number itself.

Field Note · What Changed

The "Easy" Pace Was Never Easy

For a long time, "easy" meant 5:40–6:00/km — which turns out to be almost exactly current half-marathon race pace. Every easy day was landing in the gray zone: too hard to actually recover, too easy to count as real quality work. That's a well-documented way distance runners get hurt — the fascia never got a genuinely low-load day to remodel on. Two months into real Zone 2 running, at roughly 7:20/km, with no PF flare-up since. Heart rate leads now. Pace just follows.

Heart Rate Zones

Zone 1 125 – 140 bpm Recovery. Rarely prescribed here.
Zone 2 140 – 155 bpm Full sentences, no strain. Tue, Fri, and the first ~80% of Saturday.
Zone 3 155 – 170 bpm Comfortably hard. Where Saturday's closing surge lives.
Zone 4 170 – 185 bpm Short phrases only. Wednesday's threshold zone.
Zone 5 185 – 200 bpm All-out. Race territory — not trained directly in Phase 0.

Based on a tested max HR of 200bpm (BAA 10K 2026) and a 7-month average resting HR of 50bpm, run through the Karvonen (heart rate reserve) formula. More personalized than straight %-of-max, since it accounts for fitness level via resting HR — a lactate threshold test would sharpen these further, but this is more than enough to train off for now.

§ Three — The Runs

Three run types, four sessions.

Wednesday · Quality (swaps with Tuesday for weather)

Threshold Session

Alternate weekly between continuous and cruise intervals. Always 1K easy warm-up first, optional 1K cool-down if time allows. The one clean hard session of the week — protect it.

  • Format A Continuous: 1K WU → 20–25 min @ threshold (4:48/km) → done
  • Format B Cruise: 1K WU → 2 × 12 min @ 4:45/km, 2-min jog between
  • Format C Cruise: 1K WU → 3 × 8 min @ 4:45/km, 90s jog between
  • Cue Zone 4, 170–185bpm. Hard but sustainable. No Zone 5.
Tuesday & Friday · Aerobic

Easy Run

The run that actually builds the engine — and the one that got mistrained for years. Governed by heart rate, full stop.

  • Volume 5–8 km, building gradually within the cycle
  • HR Zone 2, 140–155bpm. Non-negotiable ceiling.
  • Pace Whatever Zone 2 produces — around 7:20/km today. Ignore the number, trust the zone.
  • Swap Tuesday can trade with Wednesday if weather forces it
Saturday · Long

Long Run

Still the single most important adaptation in Phase 0 — now with a small, earned dose of race effort at the end.

  • Volume 8–15 km to start, progressing slowly across cycles
  • Base First ~80% at Zone 2, ~7:20/km. Conversational, no exceptions.
  • Surge Last 15–20% at Zone 3/low-Zone 4, ~5:40–6:00/km — today's half-marathon effort
  • Progression Grow surge duration and intensity one at a time, never both together
  • Ceiling Once long runs pass ~25K, the surge stops growing past ~5–6K — full stop
§ Four — Strength

Two sessions, different jobs.

Monday and Thursday are both kept light for now — new stimulus for the legs, and consistency matters more than load while the pattern's still new. Monday centers on calf raises, banded lateral work, and core; Thursday's short PF prehab ahead of an office day is lighter still. Both end in calf raises, because the foot is still the foundation of everything. Everything here runs on bodyweight, resistance bands, and a water jug — no gym required.

Session A — PF Prehab & Stability

Thursday · AM

Short and light on purpose — this sits the morning of an office day. Bodyweight and bands only, ~15–20 minutes total.

Heavy Slow Calf Raise — Toes Extended ★ priority 3 × 12 · every other day

The Rathleff protocol. Stand on a step with a rolled towel under your toes (this is the key — toes must be dorsiflexed). Rise up over 3 seconds, pause 2 seconds at top, lower over 3 seconds. Single leg if possible. Bodyweight on a step to start; once that's easy, hold a water jug for load. This is the exercise.

Watch demonstration
Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift 3 × 8 each leg

Hinge at the hip on one leg, opposite leg extending behind for balance. Bodyweight first, then a light water jug in the opposite hand once balance is solid. Builds posterior chain and proprioception — every running stride is a unilateral load.

Watch demonstration
Mini-Band Crab Walk 2–3 × 15 steps each direction

Small loop band just above the knees, athletic stance, step sideways while keeping tension on the band the whole time. Direct glute-med activation. Weak hips let the knee collapse inward, which loads the medial foot — an upstream cause of PF.

Watch demonstration
Single-Leg Balance 3 × 30 sec each

Stand on one leg, eyes open. Progress to eyes closed when easy, then add a soft surface (folded towel). Trains the small stabilizing muscles of the foot and ankle.

Session B — Strength & Core

Monday

Fresh legs, kept light on purpose while this is still new territory: bodyweight, bands, and a water jug for calf-raise progression once bodyweight's easy. Squats and split squats are parked for now — added back once the schedule has more buffer to support them.

Heavy Slow Calf Raise (round 2) 3 × 12

Same as Thursday — toes extended on a step, 3 sec up, 2 sec pause, 3 sec down. Two sessions per week is the proven cadence for tendon/fascia adaptation.

Watch demonstration
Banded Lateral Walk 2 × 15 each direction

Long loop resistance band around your ankles or above the knees, athletic stance, side-step against the band's resistance. Hits glute med directly. A fatigued, jelly-like burn at the side of the hip is the goal.

Watch demonstration
Plank 3 × 45 sec

Forearms on the floor, body in a straight line from heels to head. Don't let the hips sag. Quality over duration — 45 seconds clean beats 90 seconds sloppy.

Watch demonstration
Dead Bug 3 × 10 each side

Lying on your back, lower opposite arm and leg slowly while keeping your low back pressed into the floor. Anti-extension core work — what you actually need as a runner.

§ Five — PF Maintenance

Daily care for the fascia.

These take five minutes total, daily. Not training — maintenance. The foot bath is good. These additions make it better.

Morning Routine

Before getting out of bed
  1. 10 slow ankle circles each direction (under the covers)
  2. 10 toe scrunches — curl toes toward sole, release
  3. Stand carefully, then your foot bath as planned
  4. 5 min easy walking before any running pace

Post-Run Routine

Within 30 min of finishing
  1. Frozen water bottle under the foot, 5 min rolling
  2. Calf stretch: against the wall, 30 sec each side
  3. Soleus stretch: same wall, knee bent, 30 sec each side
  4. Hydrate, eat, get on with your day
§ Six — The Cycle

Two weeks build, one week back off.

Tendons and fascia adapt slower than muscle. The deload week isn't a rest week — it's the week the tissue actually gets stronger.

01
Build
Lower end. Easy 6K × 2, threshold ~6K total, long 10K. ~28K on the week — right around recent peak, just organized.
02
Build
Nudge up. Easy 7K × 2, threshold ~6–7K total, long 11–12K. ~30K on the week — a controlled step, not a leap.
03
Deload
Pull back hard. Three easy 5K runs, no threshold, no long run. Monday's general strength skipped — Thursday's PF prehab stays, light.

Then repeat — each new cycle nudges the ceiling a little further than the last. Long run toward the mid-teens (km), easy runs toward 8K, before Phase 1 build work is even on the table. Months, not weeks.

§ Seven — Hold Onto These

The principles that keep this working.

i
Zone 2 means Zone 2. Not a pace. The old pace-based "easy" zone was actually half-marathon effort in disguise. Let heart rate set the pace, every time — even when that means 7:20/km today.
ii
The surge is earned, not assumed. Saturday's closing effort grows by duration and intensity, one at a time — never both together. A controlled second stimulus, not a second hard day smuggled into an easy week.
iii
Never lift straight into a hard day. Monday's strength sits behind Tuesday's easy run and two full days ahead of Wednesday's threshold. Thursday's prehab is deliberately light — it leads into Friday's easy run, not a quality day. On weeks where Tuesday and Wednesday swap for weather, Monday's lift is skipped entirely that week rather than shuffled into the gap — protecting the buffer matters more than protecting the streak.
iv
The weekend rest block is non-negotiable. Full rest Sunday, into Monday's light lifting day — trimmed down, though Thursday's PF prehab is lighter still. This is what actually lets Saturday's long run — and its surge — get absorbed before the next loading day.
v
Load before complexity. Don't collect new exercises. Add weight to the ones you already do — bodyweight, then bands, then a water jug, then two.
vi
Consistency beats heroic. Twelve months of four good sessions a week beats six weeks of seven before the next flare-up. The marathon rewards the patient.
vii
Progress is the same effort getting faster — not more distance covered. Track 7:20/km at 120–140bpm over the coming months. When that pace drops at the same heart rate, that's the aerobic base actually being built — the one number in this whole plan that matters most right now.
